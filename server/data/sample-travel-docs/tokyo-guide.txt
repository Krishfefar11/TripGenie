Tokyo, Japan — a mesmerizing blend of ultra-modern technology and centuries-old tradition. From neon-lit skyscrapers to serene Shinto shrines, Tokyo is one of the most fascinating cities in the world.

GETTING AROUND: Tokyo's train and subway system is world-class — punctual, clean, and extensive. Purchase a Suica or Pasmo IC card for easy travel across all lines. A 72-hour Tokyo Metro ticket costs about 1,500 yen (~$11 USD). The JR Yamanote Line loops around central Tokyo connecting all major areas. Taxis are expensive — use trains whenever possible.

TOP ATTRACTIONS: Senso-ji Temple — Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa, stunning architecture and bustling Nakamise shopping street. Shibuya Crossing — the world's busiest pedestrian crossing, best viewed from the Starbucks above. Meiji Shrine — peaceful Shinto shrine surrounded by forest in the heart of Harajuku. Tokyo Skytree — tallest tower in Japan with incredible panoramic views. Tsukiji Outer Market — incredible fresh seafood, sushi, and street food.

NEIGHBORHOODS: Shinjuku — entertainment hub with the massive train station, Golden Gai bar district, and Kabukicho nightlife. Akihabara — electronics and anime paradise, multi-story arcades and maid cafes. Harajuku — youth fashion capital, Takeshita Street, and Meiji Shrine. Roppongi — nightlife and art museums (Mori Art Museum, National Art Center). Shimokitazawa — bohemian neighborhood with vintage shops, live music, and cozy cafes.

HIDDEN GEMS: Yanaka — old-fashioned neighborhood with traditional shops and a nostalgic atmosphere. TeamLab Borderless/Planets — immersive digital art museums (book tickets in advance). Kichijoji — charming suburb with Inokashira Park and excellent food scene. Odaiba — futuristic island with teamLab, Gundam statue, and waterfront views. Golden Gai — tiny bars in Shinjuku, each seating only 6-10 people.

FOOD GUIDE: Tokyo has more Michelin-starred restaurants than any city in the world. Must-eat experiences: Ramen — try Ichiran (customizable tonkotsu) or Fuunji (tsukemen dipping noodles). Sushi — Tsukiji/Toyosu area for the freshest fish; conveyor belt sushi for budget options. Tempura — light, crispy battered seafood and vegetables. Yakitori — grilled chicken skewers, best under the tracks at Yurakucho. Convenience store food — surprisingly excellent onigiri, sandwiches, and hot meals at 7-Eleven and Lawson.

BUDGET TIPS: Japan can be affordable if you know how: Eat at conveyor belt sushi and ramen shops (800-1,500 yen per meal). Stay in capsule hotels for a unique experience ($30-50/night). Visit free attractions: walking through shrine grounds, parks, and neighborhoods. Department store basement food floors (depachika) offer high-quality food samples. 100-yen shops (Daiso) for souvenirs and essentials.

BUDGET BREAKDOWN: Budget: $60-90 USD per day (capsule hotels, budget eats, trains). Mid-range: $120-200 USD per day (business hotels, mix of restaurants, some attractions). Luxury: $300+ USD per day (ryokan stays, fine dining, private tours).

BEST TIME TO VISIT: Cherry blossom season (late March-early April) is magical but crowded. Autumn foliage (November) is equally beautiful with fewer tourists. Summer (July-August) is hot and humid with festivals. Winter is mild and great for city exploration with holiday illuminations.

CULTURAL TIPS: Bow when greeting people. Remove shoes in homes, temples, and some restaurants. Don't tip — it's considered rude. Be quiet on public transport. Walk on the left side of sidewalks and escalators (stand left in Osaka). Cash is still widely used — carry yen.
