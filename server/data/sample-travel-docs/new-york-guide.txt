New York City, USA — a dense, vertical mosaic of neighborhoods where world-class museums, Broadway theaters, and food from every corner of the globe sit blocks apart. NYC moves fast and rewards travelers who move with it.

GETTING AROUND: The subway is the fastest way around the city, running 24/7 for a flat $2.90 fare with unlimited transfers within two hours. A 7-day unlimited MetroCard costs $34 and pays for itself after about 13 rides. Yellow cabs and rideshares are plentiful but slow during rush hour. Walking is often faster than a cab in Midtown during the day.

TOP ATTRACTIONS: Empire State Building — classic Art Deco skyscraper with observation decks on the 86th and 102nd floors, book timed tickets to skip lines. Central Park — an 843-acre green oasis with Bethesda Terrace, Bow Bridge, and the Central Park Zoo. Metropolitan Museum of Art — one of the world's largest art museums, pay-what-you-wish for NY residents but fixed admission for visitors. Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island — ferry access from Battery Park, book early for crown access. Times Square — neon-lit and overwhelming, best experienced once at night.

LESS CROWDED SPOTS: The High Line — an elevated park built on a former rail line through Chelsea, quieter in early morning. Green-Wood Cemetery in Brooklyn — a peaceful, historic cemetery with skyline views, far from tourist crowds. Roosevelt Island Tramway — a cheap aerial tram ride with skyline views most tourists skip. Dyker Heights in December — elaborate Christmas light displays in a residential Brooklyn neighborhood.

FOOD GUIDE: New York's food scene spans the world. Must-try: New York-style pizza — thin, foldable slices, classics include Di Fara and Joe's Pizza. Bagels with lox and cream cheese — a NYC breakfast institution, try Russ & Daughters or Ess-a-Bagel. Halal cart chicken and rice — ubiquitous street food carts found on nearly every Midtown corner. Katz's Delicatessen pastrami on rye — a Lower East Side institution since 1888. Chinatown and Flushing offer some of the best Chinese food outside of China.

BUDGET BREAKDOWN: Budget traveler: $80-120 USD per day (hostels, street food, subway). Mid-range: $200-350 USD per day (3-star hotels, sit-down restaurants, some attractions). Luxury: $500+ USD per day (Midtown hotels, fine dining, Broadway shows). Accommodation ranges from $50/night hostel dorms to $800+/night luxury hotel suites.

CULTURAL TIPS: Tipping is expected — 18-20% at sit-down restaurants, $1-2 per drink at bars. Stand on the right on subway escalators to let people pass on the left. New Yorkers walk fast and appreciate the same from visitors. Jaywalking is common and largely unenforced.

BEST TIME TO VISIT: April-June and September-November bring mild weather and manageable crowds. July-August is hot and humid but full of free outdoor events. December is magical for holiday lights and markets despite the cold and crowds.

ACTIVITIES: Broadway or off-Broadway shows in the Theater District. Walking tours through Greenwich Village and SoHo's cast-iron architecture. Food tours through Chinatown or the Lower East Side. Ferry rides to Governors Island in summer. Live jazz in small Harlem or Village clubs.
