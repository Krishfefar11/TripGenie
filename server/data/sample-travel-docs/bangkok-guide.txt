Bangkok, Thailand — a city of glittering temples, chaotic markets, and some of the best street food on the planet. Bangkok rewards travelers willing to embrace its heat, noise, and constant motion.

GETTING AROUND: The BTS Skytrain and MRT subway are the fastest way to avoid Bangkok's notorious traffic, with single trips costing 16-59 THB. Tuk-tuks are fun but should be haggled hard — agree on a price before boarding. River ferries along the Chao Phraya are a cheap, scenic way to reach riverside temples. Grab (ride-hailing) is reliable and avoids overcharging.

TOP ATTRACTIONS: Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew — the dazzling former royal residence and home of the Emerald Buddha, dress code strictly enforced (no shorts or bare shoulders). Wat Arun — the Temple of Dawn, stunning at sunset from across the river. Wat Pho — home to the 46-meter Reclining Buddha and Bangkok's original massage school. Chatuchak Weekend Market — one of the world's largest markets with over 8,000 stalls.

LESS CROWDED SPOTS: Talat Noi — a maze of old shophouses and street art near the river, largely missed by tourists. Bang Krachao — a green "lung" of the city reachable only by ferry, popular for cycling among jungle and canals. Golden Mount (Wat Saket) — a quiet hilltop temple with panoramic city views, far fewer crowds than the Grand Palace. Talad Rot Fai — a retro night market with vintage cars and food stalls, more local than Chatuchak.

FOOD GUIDE: Bangkok's street food scene is legendary. Must-try: Pad Thai from a street cart — fresh rice noodles, tamarind, and shrimp for 40-60 THB. Tom Yum Goong — spicy-sour shrimp soup, a Thai classic. Mango Sticky Rice — sweet coconut rice with fresh mango, sold everywhere in mango season. Boat noodles — rich, dark broth served in small bowls from riverside stalls. The night market food stalls around Yaowarat (Chinatown) serve some of the city's best late-night eats.

BUDGET BREAKDOWN: Budget traveler: $20-35 USD per day (hostels, street food, public transit). Mid-range: $60-100 USD per day (boutique hotels, restaurant meals, taxis). Luxury: $200+ USD per day (5-star riverside hotels, fine dining). Accommodation ranges from $8/night hostel dorms to $400+/night riverside luxury suites.

CULTURAL TIPS: Dress modestly at temples — cover shoulders and knees, and be prepared to remove shoes. Never touch anyone's head or point your feet at a person or Buddha image, both considered disrespectful. The wai (hands pressed together, slight bow) is the traditional greeting. Bargaining is expected at markets but not in malls or with fixed-price street food.

BEST TIME TO VISIT: November-February is the cool, dry season and the most pleasant time to visit. March-May is extremely hot. June-October is the rainy season with brief afternoon downpours but lower prices and fewer crowds.

ACTIVITIES: Thai cooking classes covering classic street food dishes. Traditional Thai massage at Wat Pho's massage school. Evening boat cruises along the Chao Phraya River. Muay Thai matches at Rajadamnern Stadium. Day trips to the Damnoen Saduak floating market.
