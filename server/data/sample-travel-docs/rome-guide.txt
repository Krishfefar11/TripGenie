Rome, Italy — the Eternal City — layers three thousand years of history into a single walkable center. Ancient ruins sit beside Renaissance piazzas and lively trattorias, making it one of the world's richest cultural destinations.

GETTING AROUND: Rome's metro has only two main lines and doesn't reach every corner, so walking and buses fill the gaps. A 100-minute transit ticket costs about 1.50 EUR; a 24-hour pass is 7 EUR. Cobblestone streets in the historic center make comfortable shoes essential. Taxis are metered but traffic in the old town is often faster on foot.

TOP ATTRACTIONS: Colosseum — the iconic ancient amphitheater, book timed-entry tickets in advance to skip long lines. Roman Forum and Palatine Hill — sprawling ruins of the ancient civic center, included with Colosseum combo tickets. Trevi Fountain — toss a coin over your shoulder for good luck, best visited early morning before crowds. Pantheon — remarkably preserved Roman temple with a massive open oculus. Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel — home to Michelangelo's ceiling fresco, reserve skip-the-line tickets.

LESS CROWDED SPOTS: Trastevere — a cobblestone old town neighborhood across the Tiber, quieter by day and full of authentic trattorias by night. Aventine Hill — home to the famous "keyhole view" of St. Peter's dome through a hedge, rarely crowded. Centrale Montemartini — ancient sculptures displayed inside a decommissioned power plant, almost tourist-free. Ostia Antica — a well-preserved ancient port town, a quieter alternative to Pompeii.

FOOD GUIDE: Roman cuisine centers on a handful of classic pasta dishes. Must-try: Cacio e Pepe — pecorino cheese and black pepper pasta, deceptively simple and rich. Carbonara — egg, guanciale, and pecorino, no cream despite popular imitations. Supplì — fried rice balls with a molten mozzarella center, popular street food snack. Trastevere's night market food stalls sell fresh porchetta sandwiches for 5-6 EUR. For budget eats, look for "cucina povera" trattorias away from Piazza Navona.

BUDGET BREAKDOWN: Budget traveler: $40-60 USD per day (hostels, pizza al taglio, public transit). Mid-range: $100-180 USD per day (3-star hotels, trattoria dinners, paid attractions). Luxury: $300+ USD per day (boutique hotels near the Spanish Steps, fine dining). Accommodation ranges from $25/night hostels to $600+/night luxury suites overlooking the Pantheon.

CULTURAL TIPS: Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) when entering churches and the Vatican, including St. Peter's Basilica. Many cafes charge more for table service than standing at the bar. Meals are unhurried — don't expect the check without asking for it. Sunday mornings can mean reduced hours for shops in the historic center.

BEST TIME TO VISIT: April-June and September-October offer mild weather and thinner crowds. July-August is hot and packed with tourists, though many locals leave the city for the coast. December's Christmas markets add festive charm despite the cold.

ACTIVITIES: Walking tours through the historic center's cathedral squares. Cooking classes focused on fresh pasta making. Day trips to Ostia Antica or the hill towns of Tivoli. Evening aperitivo in Trastevere's bars. Sunset views from the Gianicolo terrace.
